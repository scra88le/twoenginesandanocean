# Decisions

A log of deviations from the plan and choices made during the build. Newest first.

## 2026-11-06 — Milestone 25: the human layers, daily and monthly

Plan agreed with Anthony on 2026-11-03 (monthly factors; Climate frames of present-day use, not a 1991–2020 mean). Code and tests are done; nothing has been run against EDGAR or the real data root yet (see **Open** at the end).

- **Four layers.** `human_q` (now "Human primary power, P") and `human_ratio` are built at last, and `human_work` (useful work, `inferno`) and `human_heat` (waste heat, `amp`) are new, all log-packed like `human_q` (1e-5–1e4 W m⁻², 0.002 decades). They carry `kind` in the manifest (`flow`, or `ratio` for `human_ratio`; `stock` is reserved for the wealth layer of milestone 26) and offer Climate and Today, defaulting to Today, with no anomaly (see below). Group 5's blurb no longer says "Not available yet".
- **Monthly factors from EDGAR's monthly gridmaps.** The 2024 release publishes CO₂ per sector per month at 0.1°. Summed over 2016–2019 and 2022–2023 (recent years, without the COVID years), then per country and sector, they give 12 factors normalised so their day-weighted mean is exactly 1 (`derived.monthly_factors`; February counts 28.25 days, as in the climatology's Year frame). Components follow their sector: fossil plant losses and both electricity components the power sector (electricity is used when it is generated); fuel in industry, transport and buildings its own sector; bunkers the transport emissions over the open ocean, worldwide; nuclear and bioenergy/geothermal stations flat (they run near baseload). Factors are national, not per cell: a cell's own monthly emissions can be zero or noisy, and the placement within the country is already the proxies' job. The static file stores each month's P and useful work split into the buildings' part and the rest (`p_base`, `p_bldg`, `w_base`, `w_bldg`; 12 × 360 × 720 float32 each, the file is 5.4 MB). Tested: the 12 frames' day-weighted mean equals the annual grid in every cell, for P, work and the buildings' part.
- **Climate frames are present-day typical months.** World use grew from 10.6 TW (1991) to 18.7 TW (2024); its 1991–2020 mean, 14.0 TW, would understate today by a quarter (direct-equivalent, from OWID's World rows). So the human Climate frames are the latest energy year's months and Year frame, and the manifest gives each human layer a `climate_label` ("2024 energy use", from `clim.json`'s new `human` block) that the legend uses: "2024 energy use, typical July" / "2024 energy use, annual mean". `human_ratio`'s frames divide by that frame's 1991–2020 `bld`, as the other derived climatologies apply their formula to the frame's inputs; its info panel says the periods differ.
- **A day = its month's frame × a weather factor on the buildings' part.** Heating below 15 °C and cooling above 22 °C of daily-mean ERA5 skin temperature, w = (B + HDD + c·CDD) / (B + E[HDD] + c·E[CDD]) with c = 0.5 (cooling is electric and partly heat pumps), B = 5 K (hot water, appliances, light: weather-independent use in degree-day units) and the expectations over the calendar month's 1991–2020 normal, taking daily temperatures as normal with a 3 K spread (closed form with an erf approximation, |error| < 1.5e-7; no SciPy dependency). Tested on 200 000 sampled days: w averages to 1 within 0.5 %. Only buildings respond (about a fifth of P), so a uniform 6 K cold snap raises world P by about 8 % (18.6 → 20.1 TW in a crude test field). A day exactly at its normal is slightly below its frame (18.59 vs 18.65 TW), correctly: degree-days are convex. The constants are indicative and live in `pipeline.derived`, quoted by the info panels. Skin temperature, not 2 m air temperature, because the pipeline already has it (`skt`) and its normal is in the climate point chunks; over land at daily means the two are close enough for an indicative factor. "Today vs normal" stays off for these layers: a day minus its typical month would show only this temperature model.
- **The normals come from the published climate point chunks** (`t_skin`, 0.02 K packing), assembled once by `human` into `t_normal.nc` and kept in the store, because the daily runner doesn't download the climate point chunks (648 × 13 files) and the 1991–2020 sums live only on the laptop. Without it the factor is 1 and the days are their typical months.
- **Where the layers are made.** New dates: `build_date` adds them from `work/human/` (`human.for_build`), so they are in the date's maps and point chunks from the start and are validated with it; their failure (no grids, or a world total outside 12–25 TW) leaves them out but never stops the date. Dates already published: `human --out ./out` decodes each date's `diss_bl.png` and the `t_skin` of its point chunks, writes the four maps into the date's folder (file by file, atomically; nothing else in the folder is touched, unlike `publish_date`'s swap), patches the point chunks, then does the 13 climate frames (maps, point chunks, `clim/series`, `clim/year/series`, `clim.json`), the four layers' series, and the manifest last. It skips dates that have all four maps (`--force` redoes them). A later `rebuild` of a date drops its human maps with the rest of its old folder; a `human` run adds them back.
- **Point chunks: the new fields go after the extras.** `POINT_FIELDS` is now the 25 original layers, `t_skin`, `j_opt`, then `human_work`, `human_heat` (`layers.LATE_POINT_LAYERS`). The 27-field chunks published so far are a prefix of it (`LEGACY_POINT_FIELDS`, pinned field by field in a test), so instead of rewriting 648 tiles for every published date, the front end's `pointCodes` now accepts a shorter chunk and reads its missing fields as no data (it still rejects any other size). `human` upgrades the chunks of the dates it fills; dates before 2026-07-28 keep their 27-field chunks and stay readable.
- **The store.** `state/human/human_{year}.nc` and `state/human/t_normal.nc` (pipeline-only; `upload` never deletes under `state/`). `download_state` fetches them into `work/human/` for the daily job; `human-static --store` and `human --store` put them there, and `human --store` first takes grids built elsewhere.
- **EDGAR's monthly zips are large** (EDGAR's v8.0 page lists the monthly transport CO₂ zip at 3 975 MB, all years 2000–2022 in one zip per sector). So the fetch now streams each zip to disk (never into memory), extracts only the factor years, deletes the zip, and reduces each sector-year to one 0.5° file of 12 months (about 12 MB; `human.reduce_edgar`) before the next sector, deleting the 0.1° files: six years of five sectors at 0.1° would fill a GitHub runner's disk. The loader reads the reduced files like any others. The layout inside the zips isn't documented, so `_edgar_months` reads both a 12-step time axis and one file per month (`…_2023_7_…`); a file with neither is a year. A new manual workflow (`.github/workflows/human.yml`, "Technosphere grids") runs `human-static --fetch --store` on a runner, where EDGAR is reachable, and attaches the summary JSON.
- **A wrong turn fixed in passing:** the refactored EDGAR index first took the *first* year in a file name, which is the release (`EDGAR_2024_GHG_CO2_2023_…`), not the data year; the existing test caught it.
- **Front end, minimal:** `pointCodes` (above), `timescaleText` with the layer's `climate_label`, and the two manifest fields in its types. 160 Vitest tests pass. Playwright: 98 of 101 pass in this sandbox; the three failures were checked against the unchanged front end, where the same Climate-arrows test fails too (an older pre-installed browser build, run through a symlink because the repo's Playwright wants build 1248) and the other two pass when run alone (timing under the full parallel run). The rest of the human presentation (the cascade rail, flows against the stock, the readout's Humans row) is milestone 27.
- **Open.** (1) Run the Technosphere grids workflow (after merging, since a `workflow_dispatch` workflow must be on the default branch) and check its summary: `edgar_year` 2023, `factor_years` six years, no `default_shares` for the EI countries, `primary_tw` about 18.6, `monthly_primary_tw` with a winter peak. If the zips' layout or URL differs from the 2024 page's pattern, `EDGAR_URL`/`EDGAR_RELEASE` and `_edgar_months` are where to look. (2) On the laptop: `human --out ./out --store s3://…`, then look at a winter and a summer date and the Climate frames, then `upload`. (3) The daily job then makes the layers for new dates by itself.

## 2026-11-01 — Milestone 24: technosphere foundations

Requested by Anthony on 2026-11-01: map human energy use as Kleidon's free-energy cascade (primary power → useful work and waste heat, plus Garrett's wealth stock), filling the Humans group that milestone 11 left empty. This overrides PROPOSALS-v2's "human layers stay skipped". Plan: milestones 24 (foundations, this entry), 25 (daily layers from 2026-07-28), 26 (wealth stock), 27 (front end).

- **No anthropogenic heat-flux dataset; the layer is built from national energy statistics.** The open question of 2026-10-19 asked which gridded heat-flux dataset to use. A published dataset would give one number per cell; the cascade needs P split by source, engine, end use and useful fraction, so it is built from free inputs: Our World in Data's energy CSV (Energy Institute fuels, Ember electricity; CC BY), EDGAR's CO2 sector gridmaps (CC BY 4.0), the WRI Global Power Plant Database (CC BY 4.0) and Natural Earth admin-0 countries (public domain). Anthony has no IEA licence, so the sector split comes from EDGAR rather than IEA balances.
- **Direct-equivalent primary energy.** OWID's `*_consumption` columns for nuclear and renewables use the substitution method (about 2.5× the electricity), which counts free energy that was never taken. The cascade counts what the technosphere actually draws: fuel energy for coal, oil, gas and biofuel (`*_consumption`, which the EI reports as energy content); reactor heat for nuclear (electricity ÷ 0.33); heat for "other renewables", mostly bioenergy and geothermal power (electricity ÷ 0.33, `other_renewable_electricity` includes bioenergy); and the electricity itself for wind, solar and hydro. **World 2024: 18.65 TW**, of which the fossil store 16.25 TW (87 %), nuclear 0.94, water 0.50, biosphere 0.43, atmosphere 0.29, sunlight 0.24. Traditional biomass (about 1.3 TW) is not in the EI data and is not counted; the info panel must say so.
- **Year 2024**, the newest year the World row has every fuel (2025 has electricity only). `read_owid` takes the latest complete year unless `--year` says otherwise.
- **Countries without a fuel breakdown.** The EI reports fuels for 79 countries; the other 120 have Ember electricity but no fuel figures for 2024 (and no 2024 EIA primary energy either). Their fuel is the world total minus the 79, per fuel (coal 705, oil 3 109, gas 1 190, biofuel 7 TWh in 2024: 3.5 % of fossil fuel), shared in proportion to each country's latest EIA primary energy within ten years, else its population. World totals by fuel are kept exactly (tested); the summary lists these countries as `estimated`.
- **Components and where their heat goes.** Fossil fuel splits into power, industry, transport and buildings by each country's share of EDGAR CO2 in those sectors (CO2 shares stand in for energy shares; coal-heavy power is slightly overweighted). Power stations turn 38 % of fossil fuel into electricity; the losses stay at the plant (EDGAR power-industry grid), as do nuclear losses (WRI nuclear capacity) and bioenergy/geothermal losses (WRI biomass, waste and geothermal capacity). Delivered electricity is dissipated where it is used: 45 % at industry (EDGAR industrial combustion and fuel exploitation) and 55 % at buildings (EDGAR buildings). Biofuel joins transport fuel. Each component has a fallback chain of proxies and then the country's land area, and every fallback is reported.
- **International bunkers = EDGAR transport over the open ocean.** The 2024 EDGAR release publishes eight aggregated sectors (`bkl_*`) with no separate shipping or aviation grid, but its transport grid includes them, and cells with no land are international. Their share of fossil-sector CO2 sets the bunker power, which is taken from the countries' oil in proportion (`derived.remove_pro_rata`) and placed on those ocean cells. Without EDGAR, 8 % of oil, spread evenly by area (smoke runs only).
- **Useful fractions are indicative.** Final-to-useful exergy after De Stercke (2014) and Paoli & Cullen (2020): electricity 0.55 in industry (motors) and 0.30 in buildings; fuel 0.30 in industry, 0.20 in transport, 0.10 in buildings, 0.25 for bunkers; conversion losses 0. World 2024: **useful work 3.86 TW (21 %), waste heat 14.79 TW**. The constants live in `pipeline.derived` so the info panels can quote them; replacing them with the CL-PFU database's country values is a possible refinement.
- **Country raster.** Natural Earth 1:50m polygons are rasterised at 0.1° with a centre-sampled even-odd scanline fill (`human._fill`) and summed into 0.5° cells by exact spherical area. PIL's polygon fill was tried first and dropped: it rounds coordinates and fills boundary pixels, which added half a pixel along every coast (a 20 × 20 pixel box came out 21 × 21, +10 %). Larger polygons are drawn first so enclaves keep their cells. Codes come from `ISO_A3_EH`, then `ISO_A3`, then `ADM0_A3` (France and Norway have `ISO_A3 = -99`); OWID's `OWID_KOS` is mapped to Kosovo. Gibraltar and the former Netherlands Antilles have no polygon and are reported unplaced (3 GW).
- **A bug the conservation test caught:** `allocate` first passed nuclear electricity to `national_components`, which expects reactor heat, losing two thirds of nuclear power (a test country lost 12 % of its P). Fixed; the world total now matches the sum of the national direct-equivalent totals to 1e-9.
- **EDGAR is untested against the real files.** `jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu` (and Zenodo, Maddison) are blocked in the sandbox this was written in, so `load_edgar` is tested on synthetic NetCDFs only: it searches the folder recursively, maps `bkl_POWER_INDUSTRY`, `bkl_IND_COMBUSTION`, `bkl_FUEL_EXPLOITATION`, `bkl_TRANSPORT` and `bkl_BUILDINGS` (and the older detailed codes) to proxies, sums any time dimension (the 2024 release's gridmaps are monthly), detects fluxes from a units string containing `m-2`, and uses the latest year ≤ the energy year (2023 for 2024's energy). The download URL follows the pattern on EDGAR's dataset page (`.../EDGAR_2024_GHG/monthly/CO2/bkl_{SECTOR}/bkl_{SECTOR}_emi_nc.zip`); the page also says a newer release exists. **First real run:** `human-static --fetch`, then check that `summary_2024.json` has `edgar_year` 2023, no `default_shares` for the 79 EI countries, a bunker share of a few percent of fossil fuel, and that the map's hot spots are cities and industrial regions.
- **The real run without EDGAR** (OWID, Natural Earth and the WRI database are on GitHub and reachable): 2 s; P 18.65 TW, global mean 0.037 W m⁻²; Paris 0.94, Chicago 0.57, Sahara 0.03 W m⁻². Every country used the default sector shares and land-area placement, so this map is flat within each country: a check of the accounting, not something to publish.
- **Nothing is published yet.** `human-static` writes to `work/human/`; the layers, their encoding and the daily shaping come with milestone 25.

## 2026-10-24 — Milestone 14: pipeline foundations (proposal v2)

Done in code and tests; the data steps that wait on the CDS are listed at the end.

- **Cold side of `carnot` and `g_max` is T_r (F5).** `derived.radiative_temperature` and `radiative_summary` give T_r = (⟨ASR_TOA⟩ / σ)^¼ from the area-weighted global mean of `tsr` for the frame (day, month, year). `carnot_efficiency` and `max_power` keep their signatures; their cold-side argument is now T_r, so the fixtures are unchanged (T_s 300, T_r 255 → 15 %; R_s 200 → G_max 8.86). `t_emit` is unchanged. `j_opt` in the point extras doesn't depend on temperature. Check on 2026-10-01: ⟨ASR⟩ 243.6 W m⁻², T_r 256.0 K, global-mean `carnot` 11.65 % (it was a cloud-top map), 12.1 % of cells clamped at 0 (polar ice and night), Sahara 14.7 %, equatorial Atlantic 15.2 %, Antarctica 0.
- **`carnot_te` is dropped** (the proposal's default). `t_emit` already shows the cloud signal. Anthony to say if the local version should come back as an advanced layer.
- **The manifest publishes ⟨ASR⟩ and T_r per frame.** `manifest.radiative[date] = {asr, t_r}` for each published date, written with the date and dropped with it; `clim.radiative[frame]` for the 12 months and `year`, in `clim/clim.json`. The panels quote about 255 K; the readout will quote the frame's value.
- **`net_sfc` (F6).** `ssr − lw_up − le − h`, no mask, group 10 "Ocean", `balance`, display ±200 (the proposal's value), anomaly yes. Encode range **−1000 to 700**, not ±600: the 16 dates that have `lw_up` (2026-07-16 to 07-31) reach −842 and +500 W m⁻². Their 2nd–98th percentiles on 07-31 are −260 and +173, so ±200 holds for now; recheck on winter dates. It is **appended last** in the catalogue so the point-chunk field order of the other 24 layers doesn't change (the manifest still drives the order). A date without `lw_up` simply has no `net_sfc` (not built for it, code 0 in point chunks). The global mean of the daily `net_sfc` is −6 to +0.5 W m⁻² on those 16 dates (the range check has not been repeated on the other 74 dates, which include winter-hemisphere dates; do it when the encode range is next reviewed): ERA5 doesn't close the surface budget, which the column-budget report in milestone 15 will quantify.
- **`str` is in the daily fetch** (`fetch.VARIABLES`, a daily sum; the sums request is 8 variables × 31 days, under the CDS limit of 400). Archives fetched before this change have no `str` and build without `net_sfc`.
- **Backfill route for `str`: re-fetch (F6's default).** `fetch-str` asks for `str` alone, one request per month (cost ≤ 31), and merges it into the tidy files. 16 of 90 dates were done on 2026-10-24; **all 90 were done by 2026-10-25**. The raw `str` archives are kept in `raw/lw/`.
- **Second climatology request set, merged by key (proposal working rules).** `climatology.Part` describes a set of requests: `MAIN` (the 12 existing keys, untouched, `sums_{a}_{b}.nc`), `SURFACE` (`str`, `u10`, `v10`, `u100`, `v100`, single-levels monthly means, `sums_surface_*.nc`) and `PRESSURE` (`u`, `v` at 250 hPa from `reanalysis-era5-pressure-levels-monthly-means`, `sums_pressure_*.nc`). **Dataset ID confirmed 2026-10-24** (the collection exists on the CDS). Each part is reduced on arrival through the same conversion, and `monthly_means` merges the parts that have fully arrived (the main part is required); a half-arrived part is ignored. At most two requests are queued per dataset (the main and surface parts share a dataset, so they share the two slots). Requests in flight from before the change keep their names. 2026-10-24: four requests were submitted (two surface, two pressure); the other eight follow as slots free up.
- **Annual frame (F2).** `clim/year/{layer}.png` for every climatology layer, computed from the annual-mean inputs (the 12 monthly means weighted by days in the month, 365.25 days in all), **not** by averaging 12 derived maps; `div_e` is smoothed after averaging. T_r is the annual ⟨ASR⟩. Test: a concave formula (`t_emit`) gives a different, higher annual value than the day-weighted mean of the monthly maps.
- **The year value in the series files: published separately.** `clim/year/series/{layer}/{tx}_{ty}.bin` (one frame, 20 × 20 codes), rather than a 13th entry in `clim/series/`, so the deployed site's 12-month readout (it checks the length) keeps working until the front end changes in milestone 16. `clim.json` gains `frames`, `year_file`, `year_series` and `radiative`; `layers` lists only the layers actually published (`net_sfc` waits for `str`).
- **No anomaly without a climatology.** `encode_anomalies` skips a layer the climatology doesn't have (a date rebuilt with `lw_up` before the `str` climatology arrives gets `net_sfc` but not `net_sfc_anom`).
- **`climatology --partial`** publishes from the parts that have arrived and still queues the rest, so the T_r change can reach the climatology before the new requests are back.
- **Front end.** The menu and the point chunks pick up `net_sfc` and the "Ocean" group from the manifest; only the counts in the tests changed (25 layers, 10 groups, 27 point fields). The readout's "Ocean / storage" row is left for the readout work in milestone 21.

## 2026-10-19 — Milestone 13: deployment, URL state and the mobile layout

The keep-alive step also succeeded (its effect over 60 days is still unverified). Two setup snags, both mine and both fixed: the credentials file was created by root (unreadable to the pipeline) and lacked the `[default]` header. The next scheduled run should publish the four new dates once CDS delivers.
- **First real daily download, 2026-10-24.** A manual Daily build (`days=7`) on a GitHub runner collected the three finished CDS results for 14–17 October, built and validated the four dates (all `ok`: absorbed solar 246, OLR 244 W m⁻², rain 3.0 mm/day), rebuilt the series, uploaded 2 768 new and 14 256 changed objects, published the manifest (90 dates, now 2026-07-20 to 2026-10-17) and deleted the four expired dates' files. 15 minutes in all. It exposed one bug: collected requests kept in the state still counted against the two-per-dataset queue cap, so 18 October's single-level requests weren't submitted. Fixed (collected requests are marked and don't count; a test fails without the fix), and a rerun submitted them.
- **GitHub starts the schedule late.** The `23 7 * * *` runs started at 12:41–13:02 UTC on 21–23 October, about five hours late; harmless here, since nothing depends on the hour. (Those three runs failed on purpose: the secrets weren't set until 18:07 UTC on the 23rd.)
- **Original status, kept for the record:** everything was built and tested locally, but **none of it had run against a real bucket, GitHub Actions or Pages**. The repository has no GitHub remote yet, and no storage account or CI secrets exist. The upload and daily-job logic is tested end to end against a local folder standing in for the bucket and against fake CDS and S3 clients; the workflow files are checked for structure only; `docs/DEPLOY.md` says so at the top. The acceptance item "the daily job runs unattended for 7 days" is open for that reason.
- **Storage by the S3 API, not by provider.** You didn't choose between R2 and S3, so `pipeline.store` speaks the S3 API (any `s3://bucket[/prefix]`, with the usual AWS variables and `AWS_ENDPOINT_URL` for R2) and a `LocalStore` (`file:///dir`) with the same interface is used by the tests and for dry runs. R2 is the suggested default in the guide: reads are free. Hosting defaults to GitHub Pages (`site.yml`), needing no extra account; Cloudflare Pages is described as an alternative.
- **Upload order is the safety property** (`pipeline.deploy.upload`): maps, points and climatology, then series, then the log, then the manifest, then deletion of dates beyond the window. Unchanged objects (same size and MD5) are skipped. Tests fail the upload at the series, at a point file and at the log, each on a fresh bucket, and check that the manifest and every file it names are unchanged. A real-scale dry run (with 79 dates; the final 90-date root is 3.4 GB): the data root was 80 789 objects (3 GB) and uploaded in 24 s to a local folder; repeating it skips 80 788 of them in 19 s.
- **The daily job** (`pipeline.daily`, `pipeline.cli daily`) is the existing backfill pass run once without waiting, between a download of state and an upload. Its state download is the manifest, log, request IDs, climatology maps and the *scalar* maps of the published dates, about 425 MB in 1 957 files (3 s from a local folder); the series are rebuilt from those maps, as before, rather than kept incrementally, so they can't drift from the maps.
- **Requests must outlive the runner.** The old fetch forgot a request as soon as it had downloaded it, which is right on a machine that keeps its files and wrong on a runner that is wiped after every run: with the three requests for a date finishing at different times, the first archives would be gone before the last arrived. `fetch_dates(keep_collected=True)` keeps a finished request in the state and collects it again each run until its dates are published (`settle_state`, which also drops requests older than 30 days so a date that keeps failing validation can't keep one forever). A result that CDS has expired is forgotten and requested again. A test runs four consecutive "machines" through submit, still queued, partly done and done.
- **Credentials are checked first, and on purpose.** Measured with a dummy key: `latest_available` (collection metadata) succeeds without credentials, and the 401 only comes at the first `submit`. So a bad or expired key would have failed late, and not at all on a day with nothing to request. `Client.check_authentication()` fails with a 401 for a bad key and passes for the real one, so the job calls it first, before anything is downloaded or changed. Failures later in a run (while building or uploading) also leave the site as it was, and still save request IDs submitted before the failure.
- **A failed validation fails the run but publishes the rest.** The skipped date is logged (a `failed` row and its problems in `logs/global_means.csv`) and the exit code is 1, with a `::error::` line for the Actions log.
- **Keep-alive:** the last step of `daily.yml` calls the "enable workflow" API, which should reset GitHub's 60-day inactivity clock. **Unverified** against a real gap; the guide gives a monthly empty commit as the fallback and a monthly check.
- **The site must work from any path.** A project Pages site is served from `/<repo>/`, and the basemap GeoJSON was requested from the absolute `/basemap/...`, which would have 404'd there. MapLibre loads GeoJSON in a worker, which would resolve a relative URL against its own location, so the URL is now made absolute from the page (`assetUrl`), and the build uses `base: './'`. A Playwright test serves the production build under `/earth/` (a second preview server, built to its own folder) and checks that the map, the basemap, the data and the tour work and that no request fails; with the old path restored it fails, so it does guard this. A data URI favicon removes a stray 404.
- **URL state** (`web/src/urlstate.ts`): described in the spec. A unit test caught a real bug while it was written: a blank `zoom=` read as zoom 0, because `Number('')` is 0. Playwright checks that the address bar follows every kind of change, that a copied URL restores everything in a fresh browser context and writes the same URL back, that bad links fall back and are rewritten, and that Back isn't filled with history.

## 2026-10-19 — Milestone 10: climatology and the anomaly switch

- **No anomalies for the four vector layers** (`wind10`, `wind100`, `jet250`, `flux_e`), your decision. The spec says to apply each layer's formula to monthly-mean inputs, but the daily wind layers are the speed of the *daily-mean vector*, and ERA5's monthly means can only give the speed of the *monthly-mean vector*. Measured on August 2026 10 m wind: mean of the 31 daily layers 5.80 m/s, speed of the monthly-mean vector 4.01 m/s (bias +1.79 m/s on average, up to +5 m/s, over 57% of the globe by more than 1 m/s), so a wind anomaly would mostly show that bias. ERA5's monthly mean of hourly wind speed (6.30 m/s) is a different quantity again (−0.5 m/s against the daily layers). Left out: the switch shows the value, with a legend note and "Not for this layer", and the info panel says why. The acceptance item "anomaly mode works for every natural layer" is therefore met for the 18 non-vector natural layers (`pipeline.layers.ANOMALY`).
- **The climatology** (`pipeline.climatology`): ERA5 monthly means 1991–2020 of the 12 raw inputs, in six requests of five years (about 1 GB each; cost 2 880 of 20 000 each; monthly requests came back in minutes rather than the hours daily ones take). Each is reduced when it arrives to per-calendar-month sums on the 0.5° grid, through the same units, sign flips and conservative regrid as the daily data, and the download deleted, so about 6 GB of monthly fields never sit on disk at once. Then the layer formulas run on each month's mean inputs. Global means of the result are sensible: absorbed solar 248.5 W m⁻² in January and 236.1 in July (perihelion), OLR 239.8 and 245.8, column water vapour 23.1 and 26.1 kg m⁻².
- **Accumulated monthly fields are stamped 06:00 on the 1st and the others 00:00**, so matching by timestamp found nothing at first; fields are matched by month. The first real chunk exposed this. The test archives now use the same stamps.
- **Anomaly = daily value − the date's calendar-month mean**, packed per layer with a symmetric linear range (as specified), masked where either is. For linear layers the climatology is exact; for `t_emit`, `carnot`, `g_max`, `j_ratio` and `evap_frac` it applies the formula to monthly means, which is an approximation, and each info panel says which.
- **Fixed anomaly display ranges** (`ANOMALY_DISPLAY`), about the 98th percentile of |anomaly| over the 78 published dates, rounded (±100 W m⁻² for the radiation layers, ±25 K for `t_emit`, ±10 points for `carnot`, ±20 mm day⁻¹ for `precip` and so on), so a colour means the same on every date. - **Anomaly size:** 404 kB for `diss_bl_anom`, over the 400 kB budget, because log layers' anomaly resolution (a tenth of the display floor, 0.001 W m⁻²) was finer than ±8 W m⁻² anomalies need. It is now the display floor. All dates were re-encoded.
- **Publishing:** `clim/{MM}/{layer}.png` (12 × 18 maps, at most 235 kB each), `clim/series/{layer}/{tx}_{ty}.bin` (12 months of one tile, for the chart) and `clim/clim.json`, copied into the manifest's `clim`. ## 2026-10-19 — Milestone 8: wind particles

- **GPU particles with transform feedback** (`web/src/layers/particles.ts`), not the original webgl-wind's float textures. WebGL2 has transform feedback built in, while rendering to float textures needs an extension, so state lives in a vertex buffer: position now, position one step ago, seconds left. The update shader decodes u and v from the packed `{layer}_uv.png` field with `texelFetch` and bilinear interpolation after decoding, as the raster does. Lines from the previous to the current position, drawn instanced (two vertices per particle), leave no gaps even in fast jets.
- **Trails** fade in an offscreen texture by `0.96` per 1/60 s, frame-rate independent, with `floor()` so faint trails always lose a level and never leave ghosts in an 8-bit texture. When the view moves, the old trail texture is drawn at the map position it was drawn at, so trails move with the ground while panning and zooming instead of smearing or vanishing. The view's corners come from the inverse of MapLibre's matrix (`web/src/mat4.ts`), not `Map.unproject`, which clamps at the mercator latitude limit and would distort trails when the map is zoomed out past the edge of the world. Off-screen drawing happens in the custom layer's `prerender`, which MapLibre provides for it, and the composite in `render`.
- **Screen speed is proportional to wind speed and independent of zoom and latitude.** The top of each level's colour range (25, 30 and 80 m/s) moves at 180 px/s. Physical mercator motion would be 1/cos(lat) faster towards the poles, which would read as stronger polar winds.
- **Density:** one particle per 90 px² (about 11 000 on a 1280 × 800 map), halved under 700 px as the spec asks. One per 65 px² looked cluttered when zoomed in. Particles respawn at random in the view when they leave it, reach the end of a random life of up to 4 s, or land on a cell with no data.
- **Defaults and controls:** particles are on at 10 m by default. An Overlays section at the top of the menu has a checkbox and a level selector, with unbuilt levels disabled. `?particles=off|wind10|wind100|jet250` sets the start state (temporary, like the other URL parameters, until milestone 13). The map no longer allows pitch (`maxPitch: 0`); both custom layers draw on a flat map.
- **Every value as text:** the legend says what the particles show, and hovering shows the wind under the cursor ("Wind 10 m: 7.3 m/s from SW", the meteorological "from" direction). A date without that level's wind says "No 10 m wind for …" instead of failing silently.
- **Performance:** 60 fps still and while panning, with a 95th-percentile frame time of 16.7 ms, on this laptop's integrated Intel Arc (Meteor Lake) GPU in Chromium, at 1280 × 800 with about 11 000 particles over a raster. That meets the "60 fps panning with particles" target on a recent laptop; a 2020-era laptop still needs checking.
- **Tests:**
  - Vitest: compass directions, wind decoding, particle counts, zoom-independent speed and the matrix inverse.
  - Playwright, against new fixtures (a uniform 10 m/s westerly at 10 m and a 30 m/s southerly at 250 hPa): particles are on by default and animate, stop when switched off, show wind text on hover, switch level, report a date with no wind, and read `?particles`. ## 2026-10-19 — Milestone 7: 90-day backfill and time bar

- **`backfill` command** (`pipeline.run`): finds the newest ERA5 daily date from the CDS collection, then loops: fetch → split → tidy → layers → validate → publish each date as soon as all three of its archives are on disk. With `--wait` it polls with backoff from 60 s (×1.5, at most 600 s) until nothing is pending. Tidy files are cached in `work/`, so a rebuild doesn't re-read raw data.
- **Month-sized requests.** Consecutive missing dates within a month go in one request per statistic, which brings 90 days down from 270 requests to about 12. Results are split into per-date archives that look exactly like single-day results, so `tidy` and `build` are unchanged.
- **CDS queue limit, found during the backfill.** Submitting all 12 requests at once had 7 of them rejected ("Number queued requests for this dataset is temporarily limited"), and the old code would have resubmitted them all and been rejected again. `fetch_dates` now keeps at most 2 requests queued per dataset (`fetch.MAX_QUEUED`; the real limit isn't documented) and submits the oldest dates first across the three statistics, so whole dates complete in order. Rejected and failed requests are dropped and resubmitted when a slot frees up.
- **Queue times vary a lot.** On 2026-10-18 month-sized requests took about 1–3 hours. On 2026-10-19 one stayed queued, not started, for more than 7 hours. The daily job doesn't wait (requests are collected on a later run), so this slows a backfill but doesn't break anything.
- **Validation** (`pipeline.validate`) runs before publishing. A failed date is logged and not published. `out/logs/global_means.csv` holds one row per date and is rewritten when a date is rebuilt. Results for the 49 dates built so far (2026-07-15 to 08-31, plus 10-01): all pass. `tsr` 238.3–243.6 W m⁻², OLR 243.1–248.4 W m⁻², `tp` 2.82–3.15 mm day⁻¹. OLR is above absorbed solar in July and August, which is expected near aphelion and in the northern summer.
- **Sizes on real data:** every PNG across the 49 dates is under 400 kB (the largest is `p_lift.png` at 344 kB, then `precip.png` at 335 kB), and each date is 6.2–6.3 MB. 90 days will be about 565 MB in `out/fields`.
- **Raw pruning:** a raw archive is deleted only once its date has a tidy file. Without that rule, a date still waiting for its sums could lose its means and pressure-level archives and have to fetch them again.
- **Time bar** (`web/src/timebar.ts`): previous / play / next buttons and a range slider over the manifest's dates, the date with the note "ERA5, about 6 days behind real time", and `aria-valuetext`. Arrow keys step the date only when nothing else has focus, so they don't steal keys from the menu, the slider or the map. Play waits for each date to draw before the next 800 ms step, and stops if drawing becomes too slow to animate. Decoded layers are cached (LRU, 40 entries) and ±3 days of the active layer are pre-fetched. On wide screens it sits at the bottom centre, between 701 and 1100 px at the top, and under 700 px it wraps above the footer.
- **Running a backfill on a laptop:** a suspended machine pauses the poll loop (the 2026-10-18 night lost about 13 hours). It picks up again on its own after waking. The deployed daily job (milestone 13) won't have this problem.

## 2026-10-18 — Milestone 5: map, raster layer, legend and hover

- **Basemap:** a dark background plus Natural Earth 1:50m coastlines and country borders (public domain), minified to 350 kB gzipped and drawn *over* the data with a dark outline under each line. The data covers the whole globe, so tiles under it would never be seen. There's no tile service, API key or licence question. Place labels are not included; a labelled vector style can be added later if wanted.
- **Raster layer** (`web/src/layers/raster.ts`): a MapLibre custom layer that draws one quad over three world copies in mercator space. The fragment shader works out lat/lon from mercator coordinates, reads codes from an `RGB8UI` integer texture with `texelFetch`, interpolates bilinearly in encoded space (so log layers interpolate in log space) using only valid neighbours, and looks up a 256-entry colour texture. A no-data texel is transparent; a masked texel is semi-transparent grey. It uses `defaultProjectionData.mainMatrix`, which MapLibre documents as enough for a mercator-only layer, so globe projection is not supported.
- **PNG decoding in JS** instead of `createImageBitmap`. A small decoder (`DecompressionStream` plus unfiltering) gives the exact bytes for both the texture and the hover lookup, with no premultiplied alpha or colour management involved. This replaces the `UNPACK_*`/`createImageBitmap` approach in the spec; `UNPACK_PREMULTIPLY_ALPHA_WEBGL` is still set to false before uploads.
- **Colour scales** are exported once from matplotlib/cmocean (`pipeline/scripts/export_colormaps.py`) into `web/src/colormap-tables.json` (15 kB, 10 scales). The web build doesn't need Python.
- **Display mapping:** linear, log (decades) and centred scales (the centre maps to the middle of the colour bar even when the range isn't symmetric, as for `human_ratio`). "auto" uses the day's 2nd–98th percentile, symmetric about zero when the centre is 0. The shader and `scale.ts` use the same formula. Log tick labels are powers of ten.
- **Hover value:** the value of the cell under the cursor, with decimals set by the layer's resolution, °C in brackets for kelvin, "Not shown here (masked)" for masked cells, and "< floor" at the floor of log layers.
- **Fix found during the visual check:** log layers used to code values ≤ 0 as masked, so dry areas looked like missing data on the `precip` map. Values below a log layer's floor, including zero, are now stored as the floor (code 2) and read as "< 0.001 mm day⁻¹", and they no longer count as clipped. Masking is only for deliberate masks.

## 2026-10-18 — Milestone 4: encoding, storage layout and manifest

- **New modules:**
  - `pipeline.layers`: the catalogue of all 24 layers, with IDs, titles, groups, units, colour scales, display and encode ranges, resolutions and anomaly ranges.
  - `pipeline.encode`: quantisation, decoding and PNG packing.
  - `pipeline.publish`: encoding a date, the atomic write, the manifest, and pruning to 90 days.
  - `cli build`: tidy → layers → PNGs → manifest.
- **Fixed resolution instead of spreading each range over 65 533 levels.** With full-range steps (for example 0.008 W m⁻² for `asr_sfc`), neighbouring cells differ by hundreds of codes, the low byte is noise, and 23 of 26 files were 403–457 kB (11.4 MB per date). Per-layer resolutions about what the data can resolve brought the files to 91–344 kB (6.3 MB per date), with every round-trip error within half a step. The manifest's `scale` is the resolution, so the packing formula is unchanged. Chosen steps:
  - most W m⁻² fluxes: 0.1–0.25 W m⁻²;
  - `t_emit`: 0.02 K; `carnot`: 0.02 %;
  - wind speeds: 0.02–0.05 m s⁻¹; wind components: 0.1 m s⁻¹, enough for particle advection, and it gives `jet250_uv` room (392 → 294 kB);
  - `div_e`: 1 W m⁻²; `flux_e`: 1e6 W m⁻¹, against typical values of 2e8;
  - log layers: 0.002 decades (0.46% relative);
  - `j_ratio`: 0.002, so its ±50 range fits.
- **Vector layers are two files**: the magnitude as an ordinary scalar PNG, plus `{layer}_uv.png`. The raster path is then the same for every layer, and the front end never computes a wind speed.
- **Scalar PNGs are RGB with B = 0**, so the alpha channel only matters for the `_uv` files. Writing an RGBA PNG with A = 0 texels keeps their RGB on the Python side (tested). The browser side is milestone 5.
- **Encode ranges** are set from physical limits and the 2026-10-01 data, wide enough that real values aren't clipped. The exception is log layers: values between 0 and the encode floor are raised to the floor. On 2026-10-01 that was 6278 `precip` cells under 0.001 mm/day and 18 `p_lift` cells. They are counted as clipped in the build output.
- **Publishing:**
  - A date's files go into `fields/.{date}.tmp/` and are renamed into place only once all are written. Republishing a date swaps the old directory out the same way.
  - `manifest.json` is written last via a temporary file and `os.replace`, then dates beyond the newest 90 are deleted.
  - Tests simulate a failed file write and a failed manifest write; both leave the published site byte-for-byte unchanged.
- **Manifest contents** (version 1, about 14 kB):
  - built time, sorted dates, grid, reserved codes, and file path templates;
  - for every layer, in catalogue order: title, group, units, colour scale, display range (or "auto"), log flag, centre, vector flag, and the encode, uv and anomaly ranges with their scale and offset;
  - the Copernicus attribution text.
- **Not in this milestone:** point and series chunks (milestone 9, as the spec orders them) and the per-date validation gate (step 6 of the daily run; milestone 13 has the validation log). `build` publishes without validating.

## 2026-10-18 — `div_e` is smoothed; `flux_e` shows the divergent flux

Decided by the project owner, choosing options 1 and 3 below (evidence in the milestone 2 entry).

- **`div_e`** is ERA5 `p86.162` smoothed on the sphere by implicit diffusion: solve (1 − L²∇²) D_s = D. This damps grid-scale noise while leaving the global mean and large-scale pattern alone, and it is isotropic on the sphere, so there are no polar artefacts as with a lat-lon box filter. A wave of wavelength λ is damped by a factor of 1 / (1 + (2πL/λ)²). The length scale L is a config value (`smooth_length_m`, default 300 km at first, tuned to 100 km in milestone 3, see above), tuned so that the fine-scale share of the rms is close to that of net TOA radiation (about 10%). The info panel states L and that the field is smoothed.
- **`flux_e`** is the divergent part of the energy flux: solve ∇²χ = D_s − ⟨D_s⟩ and draw F_div = ∇χ. Its divergence is `div_e`, so the arrows run from energy sources (tropics) to sinks (poles), which is the transport the story step is about. The circulating part (k × ∇ψ in the Helmholtz decomposition) is left out. It dominates the raw `p75.162`/`p76.162` field but carries nothing across a latitude circle. The info panel says so. The global mean ⟨D_s⟩ (−0.04 W m⁻² on 2026-10-01) is removed so the equation has a solution.
- **One solver for both**, in `pipeline.derived`: a finite-volume Laplacian on the cell-centred 0.5° grid, FFT in longitude and a tridiagonal solve in latitude for each zonal wavenumber. Numpy only, no new dependency.
- **Checks** (milestone 3): smoothing preserves the global mean; ∇·F_div reproduces the input; the zonal integral of northward F_div across each latitude circle equals the area integral of the source poleward of it (exact at the discrete level for a finite-volume operator). On real data with unsmoothed D, the zonal-mean northward F_div should match the zonal mean of raw `p76.162`, which checks the solver against ERA5's own flux.
- **`p75.162` and `p76.162`** are no longer layer inputs. They stay in the fetch until that real-data check passes, then they can be dropped.
- **Rejected:** deriving `div_e` from the surface and TOA energy budget (option 2). It needs `str` fetched and ignores heat storage, and the smoothed ERA5 field stays closer to the source variable.
- **Encode ranges** for `div_e` and `flux_e` move to milestone 3. They depend on L and are set from a sample of dates across seasons.

### What `div_e` and `flux_e` should show (decided 2026-10-18, see above)

Evidence from 2026-10-01:

- **`div_e`** (`p86.162`, W m⁻²): 2nd–98th percentile is −1200 to +966, so an "auto" colour range would be dominated by noise. Its rms is 535 W m⁻², and 55% of that is variation finer than a 5×5 block of cells (for net TOA radiation, 8%). The signal underneath is physically right: zonal means have the correct sign and shape (30–60°N −24, 0–30°N +28, 60–90°N −221 W m⁻², against net TOA −51, +50 and −164).
- **`flux_e`** (`p75.162`, `p76.162`, W m⁻¹): the local rms is 2.6e10, but the zonal-mean northward flux is at most 1e9. The vertically integrated total-energy flux contains a large component that circulates without delivering energy, so arrows drawn from the raw field would show circulation, not poleward transport.

Options:

1. **Smooth `div_e`** (for example a spectral truncation at about T42, or a 5×5 box) and say so in the info panel. Cheap, and keeps the ERA5 variable.
2. **Derive `div_e` from the energy budget:** net TOA radiation minus the net surface flux (`tsr − olr − (ssr − lw_up) + le + h`, signs as in the sign-flipped fields). It's smooth, and each term is traceable, but it ignores storage and needs `str` fetched.
3. **For `flux_e`, show only the divergent part** of the flux: solve ∇²χ = `div_e` and draw ∇χ, so the arrows point from sources to sinks. It would be built from the smoothed or budget-derived divergence and computed in `pipeline.derived`.

Until this is decided the `div_e` and `flux_e` encode ranges are not set. Display-range clipping seen on this date (so milestone 4 sets encode ranges from data, not the 10% rule): `lh` reaches 703 W m⁻² (5.9% of area is outside 0–300), `bld` reaches 104 W m⁻² (1.0% above 20), `ke_col` reaches 14 MJ m⁻² (6.2% above 6), and `precip` reaches 340 mm/day (display range stops at 100).

## 2026-10-17 — Spec review before milestone 1

- **Sign flips happen once.** `pipeline.convert` flips `ttr`, `slhf`, `sshf` and `str` and renames them `olr`, `le`, `h` and `lw_up`. `pipeline.derived` never negates an input. This replaces the earlier notation (`OLR = −ttr` on fields that were already flipped), which would have flipped twice. `net_toa = tsr − olr`.
- **Package layout.** `pipeline/pyproject.toml` with the package in `pipeline/src/pipeline/`. Commands run from the repo root. Local output goes to a repo-root `out/`, which the web dev server uses as its data root.
- **Regridding.** Replaced 2×2 block averaging with a conservative remap. ERA5's 1440 × 721 point-centred grid includes the poles, so it can't be split into 2×2 blocks. The remap uses a 3 × 3 stencil with [½, 1, ½] weights per axis, multiplied by cos(lat). The 0.5° grid is cell-centred with no pole rows.
- **Reserved codes.** 0 = no data, 1 = masked, 2–65535 = valid. The no-data validation counts only code 0, so land-only layers pass.
- **Log packing.** Layers with log scales are packed in log₁₀ space, because linear uint16 packing can't resolve the lower end of the `human_q` and `human_ratio` ranges.
- **Encode vs display ranges.** Encode ranges are fixed per layer in `pipeline.layers` and are separate from display ranges. This resolves several conflicts:
  - "auto" display ranges (`div_e`, `flux_e`): their encode ranges are set in milestone 2.
  - `carnot`: encoded 0–45% to cover the 40% validation bound.
  - Layers with negative values (`j_conv`, `lh`, `evap_frac`): encode ranges extended below zero.
  - Anomalies: symmetric linear ranges set per layer.
- **`flux_e`** uses the vector RGBA packing (u in R+G, v in B+A).
- **Point and series chunks** are uint16, not int16, with the same packing as the PNGs.
- **Texture upload.** Premultiplied alpha and colour-space conversion are turned off, and textures use nearest sampling. Covered by a Vitest decode test.
- **CDS requests don't block the run.** Requests are submitted without waiting, their IDs are tracked in `/state/pending.json`, and results are collected on the next run, because Actions jobs are killed after 6 h.

## Milestone 23: ocean overlay, the Atlantic overturning (F14), 2026-10-31

- **Why GLO12 and not altimetry alone.** The satellite product (`SEALEVEL_GLO_PHY_L4_NRT_008_046`) gives daily surface geostrophic currents only: the upper limb's surface, never the deep return flow, and on any day mostly gyre and eddies. The overlay needs both limbs, so it uses GLO12 (`GLOBAL_ANALYSISFORECAST_PHY_001_024`), which assimilates the altimetry, SST, sea ice and Argo and carries them to the sea floor. The info text says it is a model.
- **An overlay, not a map layer.** The limbs are not in `LAYERS`: adding them would put them in the point chunks, series, validation (most of the globe is no data), climatology and anomalies, none of which fits a two-year Atlantic product. They have their own catalogue, `layers.OCEAN_LIMBS`, their own files under `ocean/` and their own manifest block, and a separate daily job.
- **Split at 1100 m**, fixed (`derived.AMOC_SPLIT_M`): the depth of the overturning maximum at 26.5°N in RAPID. Elsewhere the reversal depth differs (shallower in the subpolar gyre, deeper in the South Atlantic); a latitude-dependent split would need the streamfunction at every latitude and was left out. The 26.5°N figure reports the depth GLO12's own maximum falls at (a level edge, e.g. 1153.5 m).
- **Density and speed.** The transport sets how densely particles are drawn and the depth-mean velocity how fast they move, so the deep limb (slow but thick) still shows where most of its water goes. The deep limb moves at its own screen speed scale (`speed_hi` 0.05 m/s against 0.3 m/s for the upper limb), so in both a particle at the top of the scale moves at the same screen speed; the hover text gives the real speeds. Density reference: 200 m² s⁻¹ (upper), 150 (deep); above it every particle is drawn. Both are first guesses to tune on real data.
- **Density by rejection.** The particle shader keeps a respawn with probability weight / reference and parks the rest above the map (mercator y = −1) for one frame. That keeps the particle state layout (`readState`, five floats) and the wind layer unchanged; with no weight field the layer behaves exactly as before.
- **Gyres are in the transport.** The Gulf Stream's depth-integrated transport includes the wind-driven recirculation (80–150 Sv against the ~17 Sv overturning), so its particles saturate. That is honest to the field and stated in the caveats; separating the overturning part would need a gyre estimate and was not attempted.
- **Basin mask.** 0.5° cells with ≥ 30 % ocean, connected through edges to 30°W 30°N, and east of a hand-drawn Central American divide (`ocean.PACIFIC_DIVIDE`), so the fill cannot leak into the Pacific through Panama at 0.5°. Gibraltar is narrower than a 0.5° cell, so the Mediterranean is expected to fall outside; check this on the first real run.
- **State as `.npz`.** The daily 0.5° integrals (six fields plus the ocean fraction and the four 26.5°N numbers, float32, 0.7 MB compressed) are numpy archives rather than NetCDF: pipeline-only, read back only by this module, no xarray needed. Kept for the oldest published date minus 30 days.
- **Not verified against real data.** This sandbox could not reach Copernicus Marine, so the fetch (`copernicusmarine.subset` 2.5 keyword names, checked against the installed toolbox's signature), the time-stamp handling of the daily means (the request covers the whole UTC day and `load_day` checks that one time step came back), the download size and run time, and the GLO12 AMOC number are untested. A full-size synthetic day (1501 × 1441 × 50 levels) reduces in about 10 s and its eight PNGs are 50–100 kB each. **First real run:** check the log line "AMOC at 26.5 N … Sv" (expect 14–20), the basin (no Pacific, Gulf of Mexico in, Mediterranean out), and that the density references give a readable picture.
- **Tests.** pytest: `test_ocean_derived.py` (8: level edges, limb thickness, integrals and the sea floor, level-by-level reads, depth-mean velocity, the streamfunction, section transport) and `test_ocean.py` (20: regrid weights, constants and the area integral, the basin, one synthetic day end to end with its 26.5°N numbers, save/load, the window mean, encoding, the fetch request, a NetCDF read, the run's fetch order and budget, the mean appearing as the window fills, a failed fetch, dates leaving the window, ERA5 manifest writes keeping the block, the store round trip, bad credentials changing nothing), plus a CLI test. ## 2026-10-31: AMOC latitude-depth section (option 2)

- **What.** `derived.latitude_overturning` computes ψ(latitude, depth) for the Atlantic basin (the `basin_mask`, moved to the model grid) in 0.5° latitude bins from 40° S to 80° N, from the surface down, at each level's bottom edge so the maximum over depth is the true maximum. `ocean.reduce_day` stores it in the day's state (`psi`, `psi_wet`, `depth`); the 30-day mean averages the days that have it. Files: `ocean/{date}/section[_30d].json` (tenths of a Sv, null where the basin has no water, about 50 kB each); the manifest's `ocean.section_grid` and each date's `section`/`psi_days`.
- **Existing dates.** The 60-odd dates built before this have no ψ in their saved state, so the ocean job treats them as stale and downloads them again, newest first, 15 a run (`psi_days` below the window size marks them). They keep their currents and trend meanwhile; their section appears when they are rebuilt. A full backfill of the window takes about as many runs as the first one did.
- **Not corrected.** ψ is not adjusted for the net northward transport across a latitude (it should be near zero for the Atlantic, but GLO12 does not close it exactly), the Gulf of Mexico and the part of the Mediterranean inside the box are in the basin mask, and the 26.5° N column of the section need not equal the trend's figure exactly (the trend uses the 81° W to 13° W line; the section uses every Atlantic cell at that latitude). Check both against RAPID's ~17 Sv after the first real run.
- **Front end.** The AMOC panel shows the section as a canvas heat map plus the strength at each latitude, above the trend; the button is now "AMOC section".

## 2026-11-01: the AMOC "conveyor belt" (limbs summed by latitude)

- **Why.** The particle map shows each limb's depth-mean velocity at 1/12°, which is mostly gyres, the Gulf Stream and eddies, so the north/south pattern of the limbs is buried. Summing across the whole basin cancels them.
- **What.** `derived.limb_transports_by_latitude` takes the upper limb as ψ at 1,100 m (interpolated between level edges) and the deep limb as the rest of the column (ψ at the floor minus the upper limb), at every latitude. `section[_30d].json` now carries `upper_sv` and `deep_sv`; the panel draws them first, as two ribbons with arrowheads, binned to 5° for display.
- **No new download.** It comes from the ψ already in the saved state. Entries get `belt: true`; an entry without it is rebuilt from the saved days on the next ocean run (no fetch), so the 60 dates with a section gain the belt at the next run. Dates without ψ still wait for their re-download.
- **Not done (option 2).** Subtracting the gyre part from the map and averaging it over 90 days is not built; the particle layer is unchanged.

## 2026-11-01: the gyre-free particle map (option 2)

- **What.** A third averaging in the ocean menu, "30-day mean, overturning only" (`?ocean_part=ov`). `derived.overturning_part` subtracts from each limb H_limb × the whole column's mean velocity (U_total / H_total), so the depth-uniform part of the flow, which is what the wind-driven gyres and the Gulf Stream's recirculation mostly are, goes, and what is left is where the limbs differ: the upper limb north, the deep limb south, summing to zero over the column. Files `ocean/{date}/{limb}_30d_ov[_uv].png`; the manifest's `files.part` and each date's `ov`.
- **No new download.** The operation is linear, so it is applied to the saved 30-day mean. A date whose mean lacks `ov` is rebuilt from the saved days on the next ocean run (the same route as `belt`). Four more small PNGs per date with a mean.
- **Not the 90-day mean.** The proposal said about 90 days; the saved state and the window are 30 days, and a longer window would triple the stored state. The 30-day mean is used.
- **Not fully gyre-free.** The Gulf Stream and the subpolar boundary currents have real vertical shear, so the upper limb still runs fast there and the deep limb's western boundary current stands out; only the depth-uniform part is removed. There is no extra spatial smoothing. The info text and the legend say what is drawn. Not yet looked at on real data; if it is still noisy, smoothing the overturning part (a masked Gaussian of 1–2°) is the next step.

## 2026-11-01: smoothing the overturning-only view

- **Why.** On the live site the overturning-only map looked no different from the plain 30-day mean: removing the depth-uniform flow left the Gulf Stream's shear and eddies, which dominate at 1/12°.
- **What.** After `overturning_part`, each limb's U, V and H are smoothed with a normalised Gaussian over that limb's own water (`derived.masked_smooth`, σ = 2.5° = 5 cells, cut at 3σ; `ocean.smooth_part`). Land and the box edge neither dilute nor leak. Dry cells keep zero flow. The plain mean is unchanged.
- **Rebuild.** The manifest's `ov` is now a version (`ocean.OV_VERSION` = 2); entries with another value are rebuilt from the saved days on the next ocean run, with no download.
- **Open.** σ was chosen without seeing real data. If it is still too busy or too washed out, change `OV_SIGMA_DEG`. Smoothing lowers transport magnitudes (eddy cancellation), so particle density in this view may need a lower `density_ref`.
