Averaged over a year, the tropical ocean absorbs heat, and the ocean gives it up far to the north. The release is strongest along the Gulf Stream and the Kuroshio, where the surface loses well over **100 W m⁻²**. In between, currents carry the heat poleward. The chart splits the total transport between air and ocean: the ocean carries about two thirds of it at 10° latitude but only about a fifth at 35°, where the atmosphere takes over. ERA5's surface budget does not quite close, so read the ocean line as ocean plus storage.
