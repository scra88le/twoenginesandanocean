Earth is a heat engine. Averaged over a year, the tropics absorb more sunlight than they radiate away (red), and the regions poleward of about **37°** radiate more than they absorb (blue). Carrying heat from the surplus to the deficit takes work. Two engines do it: a vertical one that lifts heat off the surface, and a horizontal one that moves it towards the poles. The ocean stores heat and carries part of the load. This map is the 1991–2020 average of net radiation at the top of the atmosphere.
